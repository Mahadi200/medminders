# MedMinders — 2½-minute pitch demo voiceover (English)

Every person, organization and record on screen is fictional. Timings are targets; match them to the recording's captions.

| Time | Scene | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Title card: “Antibiotics only work when they are used right.” | Antibiotics only work when they are used right. Missed doses, unfinished courses and self-medication all feed antimicrobial resistance, and today patients, doctors, pharmacies and hospitals rarely see the same picture of one treatment course. |
| 0:10–0:22 | Home page, then AMR awareness page | MedMinders is a patient-centred antimicrobial stewardship support platform. It connects patients, caregivers, doctors, pharmacies and hospitals in one workflow, in Bangla and English. |
| 0:22–0:32 | Doctor dashboard: BMDC review badge, confirmed hospital schedule | A doctor registers with a BMDC number. MedMinders reviews it manually, and we say so; nothing is auto-verified. The hospital confirms the doctor's visiting schedule, linked by hospital ID. |
| 0:32–0:50 | Patient books a slot | A patient signs up with a password; no SMS is needed, and the phone stays marked unverified. They choose a verified doctor, a hospital, a date and an open slot. Consent is explicit: the doctor may open this record for this visit only. |
| 0:50–1:00 | Hospital confirms | The request goes to that hospital's front desk. The hospital confirms, declines or proposes a new time, and a new time only applies if the patient accepts. |
| 1:00–1:20 | Doctor issues antibiotic prescription | The doctor now sees the confirmed appointment and writes a structured prescription: amoxicillin, the dose, every eight hours for five days, an authorized quantity, a blood test and a follow-up. MedMinders never suggests a drug or a dose. |
| 1:20–1:32 | Patient shares with pharmacy and hospital | The patient decides who sees it: dispensing access for their pharmacy and the test order for their hospital, each for seven days and revocable. |
| 1:32–1:40 | Pharmacy partial dispensing | The pharmacy sees only what was shared. It records a partial supply of six capsules, and the prescription itself is never changed. |
| 1:40–2:05 | First dose → alarm → Taken → course dashboard → test alarm | The schedule starts when the patient confirms the first dose, not at purchase. When a dose is due, MedMinders raises an alarm in the open app. The patient answers Taken, Taken late, Skipped or Not sure, and snoozing never moves the prescribed time. The course view shows the days elapsed, the doses reported, and how many are unconfirmed. Unconfirmed is not the same as missed. Estimated supply is labelled as an estimate, and the app flags a shortfall only for the authorized course. |
| 2:05–2:20 | Medication problem → doctor review | If the patient feels unwell, they report it. Their prescriber sees it next to the adherence data and replies. This is support during working hours, not emergency monitoring. |
| 2:20–2:35 | Hospital releases report → patient views | The hospital accepts the test, bills in taka and releases a reviewed report. It lands privately in the patient's records archive and goes to the ordering doctor. |
| 2:35–2:45 | Closing card | Right medicine, right dose, right time. MedMinders aims to support responsible antibiotic use. Reduced misuse and resistance are our goals; we have not yet measured them. Next come SMS and push reminders, licensed interaction data, Bangla PDFs and clinical evaluation. |

## Claims to avoid on stage
- Do not say alarms work with the browser closed (they don't; SMS/push are not connected).
- Do not say MedMinders verifies BMDC or licences automatically, prevents resistance, or detects missed doses.
- Do not call the AI features working: no AI provider is connected; AI support is roadmap.
